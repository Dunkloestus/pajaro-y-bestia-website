Reheating instructions

Smoked Low & Slow. Finished in Your Kitchen.

Bringing authentic smokehouse barbecue to your family gathering, game day, or backyard party shouldn’t require waking up at 4:00 AM to tend a fire.

We cook our whole briskets and competition-style pork ribs over natural wood embers until fully rendered, tender, and seasoned with a deep mahogany bark. We then chill and vacuum-seal them whole to lock in natural moisture, intramuscular fat, and clean smoke flavor.

Follow our simple, low-temperature reheating guide below, and your table will enjoy barbecue that tastes like it just came off the cutting board at the pit.

Our preferred method

Quick-Reference Alternative: Sous Vide / Hot Water Bath

Ideal if your cut arrived in its factory-sealed, heavy-gauge vacuum bag and you want zero risk of surface drying.

  • Brisket

    Submerge the sealed bag in a water bath or large stockpot controlled at 155°F–160°F / 68°C–71°C for 2 to 3 hours. Remove, unbag, and slice directly.

  • Ribs

    Submerge the sealed bag at 155°F / 68°C for 30 to 45 minutes. Remove, unbag, and enjoy. If you wish to glaze your ribs (we do not, but hey it is your food), brush with Pájaro y Bestia red BBQ sauce and briefly broil or grill for 2 minutes until sauce is tacky.

Ribs

Conventional Oven (Best Balance of Bark & Tenderness)

  1. 1. Preheat

    Set your oven to 250°F (120°C).

  2. 2. Our preferred oven method

    • Unwrap the ribs from plastic packaging.
    • Lay out a sheet of heavy aluminum foil and place the ribs on the foil meat side up.
    • Add just a touch of apple juice and a generous pinch of azúcar morena. (Also a pat of butter if you desire).
    • Crimp the foil closed.

  3. 3. Foil boat method

    • Unwrap the ribs from plastic packaging.
    • Lay a large sheet of heavy-duty aluminum foil on a baking tray.
    • Place the rack meat-side up. Lightly brush the surface with a thin layer of warm barbecue sauce, apple juice, or a couple of small pats of butter.
    • Fold the foil over the ribs and crimp the edges tightly into an airtight pouch.

  4. 4. Reheat

    Heat for 40 to 50 minutes until the internal temperature reaches 155°F–160°F (68°C–71°C) and the meat is hot and pliable when bent.

Brisket

Preferred: keep it whole in the sealed bag, 155°F–160°F / 68°C–71°C for 2 to 3 hours, then unbag and slice. Oven backup: dish, a spoon of the juices, foil tight, 275°F / 135°C until hot in the center. Sauce on the side, after. Not before.

Ribs

Preferred: sealed bag at 155°F / 68°C for 30 to 45 minutes, then unbag and eat. Glaze is optional — we do not — but if you want it, brush Pájaro y Bestia red BBQ sauce and broil or grill 2 minutes until tacky. Oven: foil boat at 250°F (120°C) for 40 to 50 minutes, until 155°F–160°F inside and the rack bends. Do not boil them in sauce. That is not how they left the pit.

Pork shoulder

Overnight pork likes a covered pan and low heat, same as brisket. If it is pulled, toss it with its own juices before it goes in. 275°F / 135°C until steaming. A skillet with a lid also works if you stay with it.

Pastrami

Pastrami was three weeks of work. Do not microwave it. Slices in a skillet with a spoon of water, lid on, low heat, a few minutes. Or a steamer basket. You want it hot and still pink in the middle of the slice, not gray and sad.

Sausages

They are already fully cooked. Warm, do not split them. Skillet on medium-low, a film of water, lid, 8 to 10 minutes, turning once. Or 275°F / 135°C in a covered dish. High heat makes the casing split and the fat run out. That is a waste of house-made sausage.

Fried chicken

Winner Winner does not belong in the microwave. Oven 375°F / 190°C on a rack over a sheet, 12 to 18 minutes until the crust sounds right. Air fryer works: 350°F / 175°C, a few minutes, do not crowd the basket. You are recrisping the crust, not cooking the bird again.

Sides

Beans, mac & cheese, greens: saucepan, low, a splash of water or milk, stir so the bottom does not scorch. Cornbread and biscuits: oven 325°F / 165°C for 8 to 10 minutes, wrapped in foil so they do not turn into toast. Gravy: saucepan, low, whisk, a little water if it seized.

Sauerkraut

Ours is slow-fermented, not vinegar in a can. You can eat it cold. If you want it warm, a pan on low until it just steams. Do not boil it. Boiling kills the point.

What not to do

Do not microwave the meat. Do not drown it in sauce to hide a dry reheat. Do not walk away from a hot skillet. And do not tell us the brisket was dry if you reheated it uncovered at full blast. We have nothing to hide. Neither should your leftovers.
