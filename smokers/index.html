Meet our smokers

Cheech & Chong

These are not cabinets with a gas button. Cheech and Chong were built to order for this kitchen.

How they were made

The smokers were built to order for this kitchen. Cheech is the lightweight — smaller, quicker, the one we light when the cook is not an all-nighter. Chong is the heavyweight toker — larger chamber, more meat, the one that holds big meats overnight without complaining. Chong can cook at temperatures as low as 150°F / 65°C, perfect for smoking our house-made sausages. Chong can also be used with just a smoke generator to cold smoke food, like smoked cheese.

How they work

Cheech

Cheech is a reverse flow cooker. The firebox is at the bottom of the cooker and a water pan lies between the fire and the cooking chamber. There are false walls so that the heat from the firebox must travel up and enter the cooking chamber from the top. The smokestack exhaust is at the bottom. This forces the hot air to travel from top to bottom, the opposite direction as most other cookers. Thus a “reverse flow.” The meat cooks very evenly in this type of cooker.

Chong

Chong is a gravity feed cooker. The firebox is a separate chamber on the side of the cooking chamber. The fire is elevated off of the bottom of the cooker and resides on top of a fire grate. This gives a small chamber at the bottom for the air to be introduced and to collect the ashes. Carbon is stacked on top of the fire grate in a small chute all the way to the top of the cooker. The design allows the carbon to burn only at the bottom of the stack. As the carbon turns to ash, it falls into the bottom ash tray, allowing more carbon to fall down to the grate (by gravity) and fuel the fire. It allows for very precise temperature control and extremely long cook times — over 24 hours without having to add more carbon.

Both Cheech and Chong were designed to provide maximum heat control (or fire management). Minimal hot spots / cool spots, and an even flow of the hot air across the entire load of meat on all sides.

Ribs take about five hours. Sausages are low-temp smoked for five hours. Pork shoulders are overnight. Brisket can go two days. The pitmaster works the wood, the vents, and the time. It is a science, but it is also an art.

Bark is not an excuse

BBQ can be easy, except when it is not. There are not many that truly possess the skills needed to cook BBQ without creating a thick, black, burned, and bitter crust on the outside of the meat. The uninitiated call this “bark,” but that is just an excuse for poor fire management. “Bark” should only be a thin crust that is developed from the spices on the outside of the meat, not from burning the meat itself.

Sauce stays on the side — because we have nothing to hide. Sauce is often used by amateurs to cover up their shortcomings and mistakes. If you have to drown it, you did not properly smoke it.
